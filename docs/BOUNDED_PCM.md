## Thread ownership and admission

The source offers a deep copy of one callback to `BoundedPcmQueue`. Capacity is two seconds at the declared mono sample rate (32,000 samples / 64,000 PCM bytes at 16 kHz), INCLUDING a dequeued item until its sink finishes. The source scratch buffer, 5-second assembler, sealed frame copy, encryption buffers and object overhead are additional measured resources, not hidden in that bound. This is an experimental capacity, not a phone support claim.

The short queue lock protects memory operations only. A CAS on an immutable admission record containing sample count and the first close boundary orders admission against stop. An admitted copy is published under the consumer's queue lock; a copy that loses the stop race is cleared. No storage/crypto or sink call runs inside offer. The queue's source-failure callback only latches cancellation and dispatches control; it must not join the consumer, touch persistence or synchronously invoke a native stop. AndroidPcmSource routes that callback to CaptureService's independent control executor.

A single consumer calls the assembler/store. Original receive timestamps and generation are retained; dequeue/fsync time cannot reset privacy-buffer or retention timing. Queue acceptance means accepted into volatile memory, NOT saved. Existing UI already labels live counts accepted and completed counts committed. No new claim of hardware sample-position or audiovisual calibration is made.

## Stop, faults and deletion

Stop freezes the accepted prefix and rejects later callbacks. The consumer records the close boundary and drains still-authorized admitted data, seals the final short frame and finishes pause. It intentionally bypasses the new-callback stop latch only for queue-admitted samples; store deletion/generation checks still apply. Pause waits outside the coordinator monitor for consumer and discard-worker exit. An interrupted cleanup waiter retains storage ownership until exit, then restores interruption and reports it; service cleanup cannot close the store under the live consumer.

Queue exhaustion closes admission, issues one failure signal and drains the valid prefix. Finalization waits for that fault notification; a blocked sink does not prevent the producer from issuing it. The first delivered but rejected callback becomes a `pcm_backpressure` gap [acceptedEnd, observedEnd). This is a reason code, not a new product state. Later rejected callbacks and native driver losses are not thereby measured. Failure to persist accepted work remains `uncommitted_tail`. No oldest eviction, unlimited allocation, generated-silence substitution or automatic restart exists. Callback plus writer failures preserve suppressed error evidence.

Failure handling retains the original `Throwable` internally. Merging secondary failures compares object identity before adding suppressed errors, so a sink or callback rethrowing the same exception cannot trigger self-suppression and strand completion. An `Error` is adapted to a checked `Exception` with the original cause for the sink and existing cleanup callers; rethrowing that adapter is not counted as another failure. A stop callback's `signalFailure` is merged before `sink.finish` makes its sealing decision, rather than appearing only after finalization.

Consumer interruption closes admission and requests discard, but still waits for any active fault callback to actually return and then runs finalization. Interrupt status is restored after that cleanup; interruption cannot pretend the callback or sink has finished. The outermost `finally` records completion failure and releases the `done` latch even on these exception paths. `awaitFinished` still joins the consumer and any scheduled discard worker before returning or throwing, so the latch alone does not transfer storage ownership. A permanently blocked callback or sink can still leave the wait pending; there is no fabricated successful close.

Privacy discard is independent of normal drain. It closes admission and sets a discard flag even after an earlier normal stop fixed the first boundary. The request retains its local discard intent across a simultaneous startup reset; consumer, frame submission and final sealing check both that intent and the queue's irreversible discard flag. A separate memory-only worker clears queued arrays without waiting for a blocked storage sink. Only the consumer owns its in-flight array, which can remain live until the sink returns; the assembler also discards revoked pending work.

Deletion is checked before enqueue, during consumption and at frame commit. `NoteStore.delete()` signals privacy stop before waiting for the durable privacy lock; the deletion barrier still commits before key/file cleanup. Already-running I/O may complete before that barrier obtains the lock, so an early stop signal is not a completed deletion receipt. Queue-owned arrays are cleared after consumption or discard; no plaintext temporary files/logs are introduced. Clearing owned arrays cannot guarantee clearing driver, VM or runtime copies. A stalled writer can retain its in-flight buffer and leave finalization pending; never report successful save/close on that basis.

The earlier storage follow-up extended `finally` coverage around audio append plaintext/key buffers and note serialization bytes. Audio read also clears any PCM that was not successfully delivered to its caller, including a failure in the final access check; a successful return transfers PCM ownership to the caller. These are owned-array cleanup rules, not proof that immutable strings, provider copies, VM memory or persistent storage remnants are cleared. The 320 owned-buffer checks passed within the earlier `8B3E32FF` native suite; they are not an additional total outside its 1,500 checks and are not an ED140E29 runtime result.

## Durable close and crash coverage

`CaptureBoundary` carries event ID, kind, stream, generation, boot, request/acceptance times and accepted/observed sample ends. `AudioSegmentStore.beginPause(session, boundary)` records `capture_controls`, changes the session to paused with an admitted sealing limit, and records known backpressure in one SQLite transaction. Repeating the same boundary is idempotent; conflicting or stale boundaries are rejected. `finishPause` now checks deletion under the privacy lock before adding an uncommitted-tail gap, and recovery rechecks deletion after file verification and inside the control-marker transaction. Delayed finalization or recovery must not recreate controls or append new gaps for a deleted meeting. The earlier isolated-candidate `recordRejectedTail` description is no longer the implementation. These times describe host-side control, not measured hardware stopping.

A committed boundary preserves known limits through recovery. If a vanished process left the session recording without a durable close, recovery stores `unknown_tail_from_sample` at the committed prefix; unknown request/acceptance times and sample ends remain null. Recovery pauses capture without restarting it or inventing a lost-tail duration. `recoveryUncertainties` checks unlock, trusted time and deletion before querying and before return; RecordingActivity can show a missing-tail/unknown-length warning only within its authenticated NoteStore visibility lifetime and existing display barriers. This does not reconstruct the RAM queue, define an independent control TTL or complete downstream gap/report presentation. See [integration recovery](INTEGRATION_RECOVERY.md).

That NoteStore gate also guards against an unconfirmed initial clock anchor surviving a failed preference commit in memory. Its new pending-anchor indexes cover both canonical storage root and preference identity; retry uses a new durability nonce with the original time triple, and no note is saved before a successful anchor commit. Ordinary reads of a confirmed anchor add no checkpoint writes/fsync. The complete behavior and its separate failure fixture are documented in [integration recovery](INTEGRATION_RECOVERY.md); this is not a change to queue capacity or capture permission.

The 50216 note-file recovery slice added inventories of `.enc` / `.enc.bak` for discovery and includes `.enc.new` during maintenance. A read must resolve companion files; a commit must leave exactly the expected nonce/ciphertext; deletion must remove all three family members. A lone unclassified `.new` is retained with an I/O failure, while independent due-note processing continues. The scoped static note-maintenance entry bypasses only initial deletion recovery without exporting that instance; MainActivity invokes it before normal construction, which still fails closed. Existing deletion-barrier IDs are retried even if no family file remains. This prevents old cleanup I/O from precluding the sweep, not arbitrary storage failure or data loss. See [NOTE_FILE_RECOVERY.md](NOTE_FILE_RECOVERY.md) for that earlier scope. ED140E29 additionally rejects unresolved mixed companions before AtomicFile recovery and changes SQLite writer configuration; its Android execution remains pending, as described in [MIXED_RECOVERY.md](MIXED_RECOVERY.md).

Background audio expiry uses a separate scoped maintenance open without constructor recovery. With trusted time, newly expired wrapped keys are revoked in a transaction before retrying old ciphertext cleanup; ordinary store opens still perform full recovery. A stalled or corrupt old cleanup must not keep unrelated expired keys active. Clock uncertainty permits only already-authorized cleanup and still fails the new-expiry attempt. This lifecycle is detailed in [integration recovery](INTEGRATION_RECOVERY.md); it does not transfer ownership away from an active queue consumer.

Recovery, deletion cleanup and direct compaction now replay surviving range-deletion records before fallible physical work. Frame listing, read, replay admission and committed-sample counting independently exclude overlapping records with `NOT EXISTS`; stale legacy frame flags cannot bypass a retained deletion record. This does not recover a lost deletion record or establish whole-database rollback protection. The added index and repair predicate are internal storage changes, not new product states or PRD cases.
