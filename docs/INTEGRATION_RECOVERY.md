## Durable stop and unknown crash coverage

The queue's first close becomes an immutable `CaptureBoundary`: event ID, reason, stream, generation, boot, monotonic request/acceptance times and accepted/observed sample ends. A later privacy request can discard pending data without rewriting that first event. These timestamps are software observations, not measured physical stop times.

`AudioSegmentStore.beginPause(session, boundary)` verifies stream/boot/generation and atomically inserts `capture_controls`, pauses the session, advances its generation, permits only the admitted sealing prefix, and records any known rejected interval as `pcm_backpressure`. The transaction commits together or rolls back. Repeating the exact committed event does not duplicate gaps or advance generation again, including a retry after an exception observed just after commit. Conflicting events are rejected.

`finishPause()` separately records an admitted-but-uncommitted interval as `uncommitted_tail` and removes the temporary sealing allowance. It checks the meeting deletion barrier under the shared privacy lock before inserting a gap, so a delayed finalizer cannot add new gap metadata after deletion. END coverage includes a known observed overrun; committed audio remains separately countable, and coverage containing a gap is not represented as fully playable evidence.

Recovery first reapplies meeting deletion barriers, truncates bytes beyond committed segment lengths, removes unindexed segment files and, in full verification, checks retained ciphertext. Durable pending seals are completed from their recorded limits. After file verification it reacquires the privacy lock, reapplies any deletion that arrived during verification, and checks deletion again inside the control-marker transaction. This prevents recovery from recreating a deleted meeting's control records. A surviving session still recording without a committed close receives a `recovery` control with `unknown_tail_from_sample=next_sample`; unknown request/acceptance times and accepted/observed ends remain null. The same transaction pauses the session and advances its generation. Reopening does not invent an exact RAM-tail length or repeatedly advance an already paused session, and never restarts capture.

`recoveryUncertainties(session)` now exposes the existing recovery marker through checks for unlock, trusted time and meeting deletion both before the query and immediately before return. It requires an existing session and a valid nonnegative unknown-tail boundary, then returns the committed-prefix position with a null end; it does not calculate an unknown duration. RecordingActivity reads the meeting through NoteStore first, limits display to the note's visible lifetime, and retains epoch, authentication and deletion checks. A non-live recovered meeting can show the limited warning that its pre-recovery tail may be missing and the length is unknown. This is a guarded warning, not complete downstream gap/report presentation or an independently defined retention policy for control metadata. Independent control TTL and its complete cleanup remain unresolved.

## Commit new expiry revocation before physical cleanup

`AudioSegmentStore.retryDeletedCleanup(cancelled)` reclaims only already-revoked ciphertext. It reapplies meeting/range deletion records, removes wrapped keys, clears orphan `.seg` files, compacts affected segments and retries old-file removal. It does not consult time to classify new expiry and does not decrypt media.

The earlier 8B follow-up made retained range-deletion records an independent recovery defense. `recover`, `retryDeletedCleanup` and direct `compact` first replay those records transactionally, before fallible file work: overlapping legacy frame rows become `deleted=1` with `wrapped=NULL`, even if their old flags/key slot were restored. Already-revoked rows are not rewritten. The internal `deletions_by_stream_end` index supports the existing session/stream/range lookup; it does not change retention policy or add a product state.

Four access/count entry points also independently apply `NOT EXISTS` against overlapping deletion records: `frameIds`, `read`, `requireReplayable` and `committedSamples`. They do not depend solely on repaired frame flags. This protection requires the deletion record to survive; it is not a guarantee against restoring or losing the entire database, and it does not establish physical erasure. The 120 range-barrier checks passed within the earlier `8B3E32FF` 1,500-check native emulator suite. The separate host SQLite 3.50.4 probe passed 14 synthetic checks for the old flag-only-query counterexample, half-open/cross-stream/session boundaries, rollback, zero-change repeated replay and index use. Those host query results remain separate from Android execution; the 50216 note-file slice did not modify AudioSegmentStore/WAL. ED140E29 now changes connection/writer configuration and requires its own platform regression.

Compaction copies and verifies retained ciphertext, then atomically switches frame locations after writing and syncing the replacement. Surviving identity, ciphertext, authorization/retention metadata and sample ranges stay fixed. Before-switch failure preserves the old mapping; after-switch failure may leave an unindexed old file. A later call on the same open store removes that orphan without requiring a process restart, another clock decision or another session generation. Cancellation/failure never reverses a durable revocation.

`sweepExpired()` now checks cancellation and trusted time before starting a new expiry transaction. With trusted time, it atomically inserts newly expired ranges and sets their frames to deleted with `wrapped=NULL`, commits that revocation, and only then calls `retryDeletedCleanup`. An unrelated old segment's compaction, retained-ciphertext checksum or orphan-removal failure must not prevent independently expired keys from being revoked. The returned count measures newly revoked frames, not a forensic-erasure receipt. A failed expiry commit leaves no partial new tombstone/key revocation; failed or cancelled later cleanup leaves committed revocation in place and the overall maintenance attempt incomplete.

If clock validation fails, no new expiry transaction is permitted. That branch may still retry already-authorized ciphertext reclamation, then reports the clock error with any cleanup failure retained as secondary evidence; cancellation remains interruptible. This preserves clock-free recovery of prior deletions without retaining the old normal ordering of cleanup before new expiry decisions.

AudioRetentionJob uses the static scoped `sweepExpiredForMaintenance` entry. It checks cancellation before opening a store, privately opens the writer lock/database/Keystore without running recovery, performs the sweep, and closes the store on success or failure. The unrecovered instance never escapes that operation. Ordinary construction still performs full recovery and verification; the maintenance exception prevents constructor cleanup or verification from blocking the expiry transaction before the job can reach it. The job remains idle-only, best-effort periodic work: only a completed, uncancelled sweep records success; failure requests retry. It performs no decryption, capture, network access or automatic recording restart.

Clock-free cleanup grants no plaintext access and does not shorten a still-unclassified object's policy. It preserves retained ciphertext even when its current expiry status cannot be determined. Managed file reclamation does not prove erasure from flash, WAL/storage residuals, AtomicFile copies, backups or unmanaged copies. The WAL experiment uses host SQLite 3.50.4 and a synthetic marker, not real Android wrapped-key or plaintext material; its result cannot establish Android/platform erasure. [RetentionCleanupChecks.java](../android/tests/RetentionCleanupChecks.java) exercises transaction boundaries, same-instance orphan retry, cancellation, retained evidence and clock-independent cleanup. The old APK failed its newly added cleanup assertion 58 in `integration-expiry-red.log`: an old compaction failure prevented independent expired-key revocation. The expanded 107-check suite passed on historical `5E5572ED` and `8B3E32FF`; current-candidate Android regression is pending.

## Note companion files and independent maintenance

NoteFileSet treats `.enc`, `.enc.bak` and `.enc.new` as one file family. Discovery includes base/backup candidates; maintenance additionally inventories pending files. A lone `.new` is not admitted as a saved note or silently discarded. Read recovery must leave a regular base with no companions before decrypting. After `finishWrite`, direct base-file comparison must match the intended nonce/ciphertext exactly and find no trailing bytes or companions; it does not call recovery again and accidentally accept an old backup. Deletion checks that all three paths are absent after AtomicFile cleanup. Failed postconditions report I/O failure instead of successful read/save/delete.

The scoped static `NoteStore.sweepExpired(context, cancelled)` privately constructs a store without initial deletion recovery and never exports that instance. MainActivity invokes it on its worker before ordinary construction. The sweep first retries durable deletion-barrier IDs, even when no file remains, then inventories all family members. Per-object I/O failures are retained while independent candidates continue; the scan ultimately reports those failures rather than a false overall success. Cancellation, authentication loss, uncertain time and invalid policy still stop processing. Normal constructors retain fail-closed recovery. This closes the entry-path defect where old cleanup failure prevented the sweep from starting; the independent 291-check host harness does not directly test that entry, and the 98 added native checks are compiled only. See [NOTE_FILE_RECOVERY.md](NOTE_FILE_RECOVERY.md) for exact implementation and evidence.

These checks do not set a new retention policy, manufacture a deadline for an ambiguous `.new`, or promise forensic removal from platform/flash/backup copies. That 50216 note implementation changed without modifying AudioSegmentStore or WAL. The later ED140E29 mixed-copy and writer-policy changes are documented in [MIXED_RECOVERY.md](MIXED_RECOVERY.md).

## Owned plaintext and undelivered PCM

Audio append now places plaintext conversion, key generation and encryption inside cleanup coverage: its owned plaintext and generated DEK arrays are cleared in `finally`, including early failures. Audio read keeps DEK, decrypted bytes and PCM in one outer cleanup scope; unsuccessful delivery clears the PCM even when the final access check throws. A successful return transfers PCM ownership to the caller instead of clearing the returned samples.

Note serialization bytes are cleared in `finally` across size checks, key access, cipher initialization, AAD and encryption. The existing decrypted note-byte cleanup also remains around parsing. These rules cover owned mutable arrays, not every immutable Java string, parsed object, cryptographic-provider copy, VM copy or AtomicFile/WAL remnant. ## Note clock gate precedes body access

`NoteStore.read()` checks unlock, identifier syntax, deletion and retention time before reading the encrypted body or initializing decryption. Known clock uncertainty therefore blocks file-body reading, decryption and parsing. After decryption, identity/retention validation and expiry cleanup, it rechecks unlock, deletion and time before returning. If a deadline passes during rewrite or a delayed return, it filters again rather than returning superseded content. Key/authentication and write failures remain fail-closed.

The note clock requires a complete, correctly typed wall/elapsed/boot anchor with valid values. Missing fields are not silently filled. A new anchor can be initialized only when both its preference record and the meeting directory are empty; AtomicFile backup/new files also count as existing content. Initialization must commit before creation. Existing data with missing provenance is refused, not assigned a fresh lifetime.

A failed SharedPreferences commit can nevertheless replace its in-memory map. NoteStore therefore registers the unconfirmed initial anchor before editing preferences in two process-wide indexes: canonical meeting-directory path and SharedPreferences object identity, both protected by the privacy lock. A false return or exception leaves that barrier in place across NoteStore instances; a complete cached triple alone cannot authorize creation. Conflicting indexes, a changed preference object/root pairing, changed or partial cached values, or unexpected stored content are rejected. No note key or encrypted note is created until the initial anchor has been confirmed by a successful synchronous commit.

An allowed retry preserves the original wall/elapsed/boot triple, including after the failed cache is reloaded as empty. It revalidates same-boot time and empty meeting storage, and changes only a `durability_nonce` to ensure a fresh preference edit before retrying the commit. Success clears both pending indexes; failure leaves both in place. It does not reset the origin to retry time or extend a lifetime. These pending records are an internal process-memory durability barrier, not a new product state or cross-process rollback guarantee.

For a valid same-boot anchor, current time is the monotonic projection:

```text
projected = anchor.wall + (current.elapsed - anchor.elapsed)
```

Normal reads of a confirmed anchor validate and return that projection without preference commits or checkpoint fsync. The initial commit/retry is the exception, not a recurring read-time durability write. Wall time must agree within two seconds of sampling tolerance; larger forward or backward jumps cause quarantine. A slightly ahead wall clock within tolerance does not advance the stored anchor or the returned projection. Negative values, elapsed rollback, overflow and cross-boot reuse are rejected.

The same conservative policy underlies audio retention. An unverified forward jump is no longer persisted as a later anchor that could delete content early. This is the current behavior; [CONCURRENCY_HARDENING.md](CONCURRENCY_HARDENING.md) describes the prior checkpoint implementation. Avoiding routine checkpoint writes does not make every read I/O-free: migration, expiry barriers and encrypted cleanup can still write.

The existing synthetic retention envelope remains separate from clock trust. Its development snapshot is not a user's production policy choice; legacy deadlines are not rebased. Production per-layer TTL, consent-declaration retention, trusted cross-boot recovery and the maximum allowed clock-uncertainty isolation period remain unresolved. Refusing access is not a claim that indefinite retention has been approved.
